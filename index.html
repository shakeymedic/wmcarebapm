Pre-hospital Management of Extreme Preterm Birth

A Summary for Pre-hospital Emergency Medicine Professionals

Based on the BAPM Framework for Practice (Feb 2022)

Introduction & Core Principles

The unplanned, out-of-hospital birth of an extremely preterm baby is a rare but critically challenging event. This guidance provides a pragmatic framework for management, acknowledging the limitations of the pre-hospital environment. The primary goals are to provide safe, effective, and compassionate care to both mother and baby, ensuring a smooth transition to definitive hospital care.

Key Change in Practice:

This guidance advises that for a baby born before 22+0 weeks' gestation, resuscitation should not be attempted. This is a change from previous JRCALC guidelines.

Maternal Priority:

If the mother is clinically unstable, her care takes precedence over the care of the extremely preterm baby, especially when personnel are limited.

1. Correct Assessment

Determine gestation (<22 wks vs ≥22 wks). Prioritise the mother if unstable. Prepare for complications.

2. Correct Management

Deliver timely, appropriate interventions. This will be either survival-focused care or comfort-focused care.

3. Correct Destination

Access the most appropriate neonatal and maternal expertise, keeping mother and baby together if possible.

4. Good Communication

Use sensitive, clear, and empathetic language. The words used will have a lasting impact on parents.

Management Pathways at Birth

The initial management decision is based on the confirmed or estimated gestational age.

Comfort-Focussed (Palliative) Care

Indication: Gestation is known to be < 22 completed weeks (≤21+6).

Resuscitation should not be attempted. The focus is on providing comfort to the baby and emotional support for the family.

  • Explain to parents that the baby may show signs of life (gasping, movement).
  • Facilitate parents holding the baby (skin-to-skin or swaddled).
  • Use a "cuddle pocket" or family blanket if available.
  • Offer memory-making opportunities (e.g., photos).
  • Transport mother and baby together to maternity care.

Active, Survival-Focussed Care

Indication: Gestation is ≥ 22+0 weeks or is uncertain.

Commence simple interventions to support the baby until specialist neonatal assessment is possible. If in doubt about signs of life, commence this pathway.

  • Address three key areas immediately:
    1. Thermal Care
    2. Airway & Breathing Support
    3. Transit to Definitive Care

Detailed Procedures for Survival-Focussed Care

Extremely preterm babies lose heat rapidly. Hypothermia worsens prognosis.

  • Prepare Environment: Close doors/windows, increase ambient heat.
  • Polythene Bag: Immediately at birth, place baby feet-first into a food-grade (or clinical) polythene bag up to the neck. Do not dry the baby first.
  • Hat: Place a hat on the baby's head.
  • Swaddle: Wrap the baby (still in the bag) in a warm, dry towel or blanket.
  • Heated Mattress: Place the swaddled baby on a heated mattress if available (ensure baby is wrapped and not in direct contact).
  • Deferred Cord Clamping: Unless maternal haemorrhage is a concern, wait 60 seconds before clamping the cord. Use this time to perform the thermal care steps.
  • Maintain head in a neutral position and provide jaw support.
  • Gently stimulate the baby to breathe.
  • Assess: If poor/absent respiratory effort, provide support. If no heart rate and no respiratory effort, still proceed with respiratory support initially.
  • Ventilation: Use the smallest mask available. Provide 5 gentle inflation breaths (each sustained over 2-3 seconds).
  • Follow with gentle ventilation breaths at approx. 30 breaths per minute in room air.
  • Effective Ventilation Indicator: An adequate heart rate (>100 bpm) is the best indicator. Chest wall rise is difficult to assess and may require unwrapping the baby, risking heat loss. Auscultate heart rate over the bag/blanket.
  • Chest Compressions: NOT recommended for babies <24+0 weeks' gestation, even with a very slow (<60 bpm) or undetectable heart rate, as they are unlikely to be helpful. For babies >24 weeks or unknown gestation, follow standard NLS guidelines (3:1 ratio).
  • Continue Support: Move mother and baby to the ambulance, continuing uninterrupted airway/breathing support. This is best done on a flat surface.
  • Optimise Thermal Care: Keep baby swaddled on heated mattress. Maximise ambulance temperature.
  • Pre-alert: Place an early and detailed pre-alert call to the receiving unit.
  • Destination: Transport to a facility with appropriate neonatal and maternal expertise, even if it means bypassing a closer hospital. The recommended destination is the labour ward, not the Emergency Department.
  • Keep Together: Transport mother and baby together whenever possible.

Reconsidering Resuscitation

If, despite airway and breathing support, no heartbeat is detectable with a stethoscope on at least two occasions 10 minutes apart, and arrival at hospital is not imminent, it is reasonable to reconsider resuscitation attempts.

This decision should be made in discussion with the parents if possible, leading to a reorientation of care towards comfort-focused measures.

If there is any doubt, continue ventilation until arrival at hospital.

Communication & Staff Support

Sensitive communication is vital.

  • Use simple, clear language. Avoid medical jargon.
  • Mirror the parents' language (e.g., if they say "baby").
  • Do not use the terms "abortion" or "miscarriage".
  • Acknowledge the significance of the loss.

Attending these events is emotionally challenging. Seek support through your trust's services and participate in multidisciplinary debriefs.
